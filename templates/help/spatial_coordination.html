Spatial coordination

Two panes. The one you are coordinating from stays put while you go looking.

The split pane

Pane 1 is anchored. It holds the sheet and the selection you started from, and nothing pane 2 does may move it. That is the whole property: a coordination view where you do not lose your place while checking something else.

Each pane holds its own zoom, pan and rotation. They are deliberately not linked — two sheets at different scales, snapped together, would imply an alignment nobody measured.

Why zoom never goes soft

Panes serve vectors, not pictures. A sheet is one SVG, and a crop is a different viewBox onto that same file — four numbers, not a second image.

Two consequences worth knowing. Linework stays sharp at any magnification, because there is no resolution to run out of. And a crop cannot disagree with the full sheet about what the drawing says, because there is only one drawing: framing a region changes what you are looking at, never what is there.

Thumbnails are still pictures. A 175 px tile has no zoom, and parsing 57,906 paths to fill one would be wasted work.

Callouts

A callout reading 1/A801 names its own target. Pressing it frames that detail in pane 2 and does not touch pane 1.

If you press something that looks like a control and nothing happens, the desk notices and offers the sheet the callout names. It does not guess: the suggestion is read off the drawing, never inferred, so it can only ever offer a target that is genuinely there.

Where a relationship came from

Every stated relationship between two sheets carries a basis, and the three are told apart by form as well as colour, so they survive a greyscale print:

Direct
The sheet itself carries the reference — a callout bubble, a matching room number, a shared dimension.
Inferred
Plausible from the set, but not drawn on the sheet. Shown, and labelled as inference rather than fact.
Unresolved
Named somewhere, but the target is not in the material. Shown rather than hidden — a missing discipline is often the most useful thing on the screen.

A discipline the cover index names but that delivered no sheets still appears, marked unresolved. A project page showing only the disciplines that happen to have files would hide exactly the fact somebody needs.

Sheet orientation

Native orientation is derived, then applied as an additional rotation on top of whatever the file already declares — not as an absolute one. Treating it as absolute undoes a publisher's own correct rotation and stands upright sheets on end.

Reset returns to that derived orientation. Rotating a pane is a way of looking; it never edits the source PDF.
