Building Box in meetings

Running a shanty or trailer meeting from a Building Box pass.

Two layers, and only one of them is ours

A meeting has a conversation layer — whatever video or phone bridge you already use — and a drawing layer, which is this. ARCHIOSK deliberately does not host the call. Nothing here captures audio, video, or a participant list.

Today the two are joined by screen sharing and by everyone holding their own pass. That is less clever than it sounds, and it has one large advantage: every person sees only what their own pass permits, even while looking at the same drawing.

Before the meeting

  1. Open the access panel for the project.
  2. Issue one pass per attending party. Give each the narrowest role that lets them do their job — a framing sub needs structural, not the architectural set.
  3. Copy each link as you create it. It is shown once and cannot be recovered.
  4. Set the expiry to the length of the meeting, or the day.

In the trailer — QR onboarding

The point of the QR is that nobody types a token on a site.

  1. On the access panel, press Re-issue QR on the row for the party in front of you. A Building Box field pass appears with a scannable code.
  2. Turn the laptop around. They scan it with the phone camera — no app, no account.
  3. They land on their own sheet directory, scoped to their own disciplines.

Re-issue kills the previous link. That is intentional: the usual reason to re-issue is that the first one went somewhere it should not have. If they already have a working link, do not press it.

Print placard produces a single sheet carrying just the code, the scope and the expiry — taped to a site box, that is the pass. The printed page deliberately excludes the form and the token table, which would otherwise be a credential list left on a table.

During the meeting

Share the presenter's screen for the discussion, and let people open their own pass on their own phone for anything they need to look at closely. Zoom and pan are per-device; nobody is dragged around by somebody else's scrolling.

When the assistant cannot answer something, it says so and files the question — verbatim, with the sheet and the view — for the project's architect. That is a better outcome in a meeting than a confident guess nobody can check.

After

Revoke the passes. It takes effect on the next request, and the row stays in the table so the record of who had access survives.

Live shared navigation — not built

Synchronised presenter/follower navigation — a lead room whose view others follow, free-roam and re-centering, smoothed cursor ghosts — does not exist in this product. It is described here only so nobody plans a meeting around it.

It needs a live relay between browsers. That conflicts with two constraints this project has deliberately settled: the application runs threaded gthread workers rather than an async event loop, and the deployment is a single server with no queue or worker infrastructure. Both are recorded decisions, not oversights, and reversing either is an architectural change with its own authorisation rather than a feature that can be added quietly.

Until then, screen sharing carries the shared view and passes carry the private ones.
