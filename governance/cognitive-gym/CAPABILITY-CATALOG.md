# GO Cognitive Gym — working capability catalog

**Status:** ACCEPTED FOUNDATION, version 1.0. Product Owner authority:
`GO COGNITIVE GYM — FOUNDATION REGISTRATION 01`, registered 2026-09-08.
This records the accepted prior conversational catalog, not newly measured GO
performance. [Foundation and evaluator specification](README.md) governs its
use. All capabilities start UNTESTED in this register; no historical AFT outcome
is imported as a cognitive level.

These are 28 operational assessment bars, not 28 biologically independent
faculties. No human IQ or developmental-age equivalence is asserted. Families
organize the catalog; neither family scores nor a synthetic intelligence total
may be averaged from them. IDs are stable. A domain skill alone does not justify
a new bar. Dependencies between bars must remain visible when attributing an
observed difficulty.

## Six organizing families

| Family | Bars |
|---|---|
| PERCEPTION AND REPRESENTATION | C01, C02, C03, C04, C05, C06, C09 |
| FORMAL AND EXPLANATORY REASONING | C07, C08, C10, C15, C16, C17, C18 |
| TIME, MEMORY, AND ADAPTATION | C11, C12, C13, C14 |
| AGENCY AND INTERACTION | C19, C20, C21, C22 |
| CREATION AND INTERPRETATION | C23, C24, C25 |
| EPISTEMIC CONTROL AND GOVERNANCE | C26, C27, C28 |

## Capability definitions and observable bars

The third column defines evidence of competence, not current competence. The
fourth identifies developmental information, not terminal cognitive failure.

| ID | Accepted name and plain-language definition | Evidence of competence | Development frontier / confounders to distinguish |
|---|---|---|---|
| C01 | **Attention and inhibition:** select relevant information and resist distractions or premature responses. | Uses relevant cues and changes attention when the task changes. | Salience capture, missed details, perseveration; distinguish input visibility and search load. |
| C02 | **Visual form recognition:** distinguish shapes, objects, boundaries, parts, and occlusions. | Identifies visible structure without inventing hidden detail. | Similar-form confusion or invented completion; distinguish resolution, rendering, scale, and occlusion. |
| C03 | **Perceptual organization / Gestalt:** recognize grouping, figure-ground, hierarchy, continuity, balance, and negative space. | Describes supported organization and acknowledges plausible alternative groupings. | Grouping mistaken for meaning; distinguish perceptual observation from taste and semantic interpretation. |
| C04 | **Pattern recognition and abstraction:** identify relationships or invariants across changing appearances. | Recognizes rules that survive irrelevant variation and detects counterexamples. | Surface memorization, overfitting, unjustified unique rules; distinguish prior exposure and underdetermination. |
| C05 | **Orientation and navigation:** maintain heading, position, routes, origin, and reference frames through movement. | Integrates routes/landmarks, returns toward origin, changes frames, and bounds ambiguous location. | Heading-position confusion or lost origin; distinguish memory load, missing cues, and map conventions. |
| C06 | **Spatial and geometric reasoning:** reason about shape, distance, topology, containment, intersection, and transformations. | Preserves spatial relations through views, rotation, projection, folding, and scale changes. | Adjacency treated as connection or projection as depth; distinguish missing scale and measurement error. |
| C07 | **Numerical and quantitative reasoning:** reason about amount, order, proportion, units, and magnitude. | Calculates or estimates appropriately and checks dimensions and plausible scale. | Arithmetic, ratio, unit, or magnitude errors; distinguish numeral perception and calculator access. |
| C08 | **Logic and constraint reasoning:** determine what follows from premises and which combinations are possible. | Distinguishes entailment, contradiction, possibility, and insufficient premises. | Reversed implication or plausible inference treated as proof; distinguish ambiguous language and incomplete premises. |
| C09 | **Symbolic meaning and reference binding:** separate what a symbol signifies from its particular referent. | Keeps IDENTITY and TARGET distinct and binds a reference to its local subject. | Local codes contaminate meaning or properties bind to the wrong target; distinguish illegibility and unspecified conventions. |
| C10 | **Mathematical modeling and proof:** express structure mathematically and justify conclusions under assumptions. | Selects suitable models, checks assumptions, and gives valid derivations or counterexamples. | Unsuitable formula, examples treated as proof, changed assumptions; distinguish prerequisite mathematics and tools. |
| C11 | **Temporal reasoning:** reason about order, duration, simultaneity, change, and historical versus current state. | Preserves chronology and temporal scope; distinguishes what was true from what remains true. | Old requests treated as present status; distinguish missing timestamps and document/event dates. |
| C12 | **Memory and governed state carry:** retain, retrieve, update, and use only legitimately available state. | Carries selected information with provenance and assesses current local evidence independently. | Forgotten, excluded, or stale carry; distinguish transport/storage defects from reasoning and recall. |
| C13 | **Learning flexibility and updating:** revise acquired rules when relevant evidence warrants it. | Learns from permitted feedback, detects change, and preserves unaffected knowledge. | Perseveration or wholesale overcorrection; distinguish feedback quantity and hidden persistence. |
| C14 | **Analogy and cross-domain generalization:** transfer relational structure while recognizing its limits. | Applies useful invariants in new settings without importing irrelevant properties. | Surface analogy or overextension; distinguish knowledge gaps and familiarity. Analogy and generalization retain separate sub-results. |
| C15 | **Causal and counterfactual reasoning:** distinguish causes from associations and reason about interventions. | Separates observation/intervention and identifies alternative explanations and counterfactual limits. | Correlation promoted into cause; distinguish confounding and genuine non-identifiability. |
| C16 | **Physical and mechanistic reasoning:** predict behavior from components, interactions, and supplied physical rules. | Traces mechanisms and respects relevant physical constraints. | Impossible mechanism or familiar intuition overriding supplied rules; distinguish physical knowledge and unspecified conditions. |
| C17 | **Systems and adaptive-system reasoning:** understand feedback, dependencies, emergence, thresholds, and interacting populations. | Traces indirect effects and distinguishes local from system behavior. | Linear extrapolation or ignored feedback/delay; distinguish model omissions and hidden initial conditions. |
| C18 | **Scientific inquiry:** formulate testable explanations and choose observations that distinguish them. | Designs discriminating comparisons, controls confounders, and updates explanations. | Non-discriminating experiments or protected favored explanation; distinguish apparatus knowledge and unavailable measurements. |
| C19 | **Planning and control:** choose and revise actions toward an objective under constraints. | Produces feasible sequences, manages dependencies, and replans after change. | Impossible ordering or wrong objective; distinguish goal ambiguity, horizon, and simulator fidelity. |
| C20 | **Strategic, multi-actor, and adversarial reasoning:** plan while other actors pursue their own objectives. | Anticipates incentives, responses, sacrifice, coalitions, incomplete information, and changing goals. | Passive-opponent assumptions or invented intentions; distinguish opponent strength, chance, and payoff assumptions. |
| C21 | **Social reasoning and perspective taking:** distinguish actors' knowledge, beliefs, intentions, and observations. | Maintains separate perspectives and coordinates without assuming shared knowledge. | Omniscient reasoning or stereotyped motives; distinguish cultural assumptions and ambiguous intent. |
| C22 | **Language and pragmatic reasoning:** interpret and communicate meaning, reference, implication, and ambiguity. | Preserves meaning and qualifications and recognizes context-dependent interpretations. | Lost qualifications or unsupported disambiguation; distinguish vocabulary, language proficiency, and conventions. |
| C23 | **Creativity and constructive synthesis:** generate varied, original, useful possibilities under constraints. | Produces genuinely different options satisfying an evaluable brief. | Cosmetic variation, unusable novelty, or ignored constraints; distinguish fluency from diversity and prior exposure. |
| C24 | **Artistic and nonliteral interpretation:** relate observed form to possible expressive meaning. | Supports interpretations with features and separates observation, intention, interpretation, and preference. | Taste asserted as fact or invented artist intent; distinguish culture, art knowledge, and evaluator disagreement. |
| C25 | **Auditory and musical structure:** recognize rhythm, relative pitch, motifs, layering, and temporal form. | Tracks structure through variation and distinguishes heard sound from notation knowledge. | Surface-only matching or lost rhythm; distinguish audio availability, encoding, and musical familiarity. |
| C26 | **Uncertainty and probabilistic reasoning:** represent what is known, likely, ambiguous, or unidentifiable. | Calibrates confidence, updates with evidence, and abstains selectively. | Unsupported certainty or blanket hesitation; distinguish base rates and incentives that reward abstention. |
| C27 | **Metacognition and error monitoring:** assess and regulate the reliability of one's own output behavior. | Detects errors, chooses effective checks, and revises when evidence warrants it. | Self-assurance without checking or changing correct answers under pressure; distinguish hindsight and feedback leakage. |
| C28 | **Evidence discipline and governed restraint:** connect consequential claims to sufficient permitted evidence and authority. | States narrow supported conclusions and separates evidence, inference, proposal, and authorized decision. | Citation-as-proof, out-of-scope evidence, or excessive authority; distinguish formatting from substantive support. |

## Scope of the bars

- Orientation includes heading, relative position, landmark use, route
  integration, return to origin, frame changes, and navigation without labels.
  Birds and other navigational animals inspire abstract tasks; GO is not
  credited with biological senses or bodily mechanisms it does not possess.
- Strategy includes second-order consequences, delayed advantage, coalitions,
  incomplete information, and changing objectives. Competitive, cooperative,
  and adversarial sub-results remain distinct. Political material must be
  synthetic and non-partisan; ideology is not the assessment target.
- Artistic observation includes balance, tension, repetition, rhythm, hierarchy,
  grouping, intentional asymmetry, contrast, negative space, implied movement,
  and nonliteral resemblance. Interpretation need not have one correct aesthetic
  answer. Future art material is original synthetic work, not created here.
- Science realms include mathematics, geometry, physics, chemistry, biology,
  ecology, astronomy, and engineering. Terms and facts are knowledge; inference
  with those facts is cognition. Biological/ecological systems fit especially
  C15/C17/C18; astronomical scale and frames draw on C05/C06/C07/C11/C16/C26.
- Most bars admit visual or diagram-based assessment. C22 inherently concerns
  language; visual substitutes do not establish full auditory C25 competence.
  C12/C13 and adaptive C19/C20 need interaction for full assessment. Static
  descriptions of time do not prove maintained state during actual interaction.
- Transfer is measured for every bar, not only C14. A multi-capability task names
  its primary target and secondary demands; it cannot automatically establish
  all capabilities it happens to involve.

## Research lineage (inspiration, not GO performance evidence)

The accepted taxonomy drew on executive-function research
([Diamond](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-113011-143750)), perceptual grouping
([Wagemans et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3482144/)), bird route
learning ([homing-pigeon study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1634935/)),
cooperative marine-mammal behavior
([Jaakkola et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6170804/)), children's
causal learning ([Gopnik et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14756583/)),
multi-actor strategy ([Diplomacy study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36413172/)),
tone-sequence learning ([Saffran et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10193055/)),
selective uncertainty behavior ([Smith et al., one dolphin studied](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8530911/)),
and generalization under specified priors
([Chollet](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.01547)). These references preserve the
previous research lineage; this registration performs no new empirical study
and does not infer subjective awareness from behavioral performance.
