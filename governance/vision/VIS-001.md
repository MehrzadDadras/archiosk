## Vision statement

> ARCHIOSK is the kiosk: professional design intelligence is brought out of the
> traditional studio and made accessible through a simple interface.

## Product intent

Professional design and construction intelligence normally lives inside a studio —
reachable only by knowing which specialist to ask, and when. ARCHIOSK's intent is
to put that intelligence behind a counter a non-specialist can walk up to, without
diluting what sits behind the counter.

The governing record states the path directly:

```
USER → COMPOSER → GO → appropriate intelligence, evidence, tools and workflows
```

This holds at all three levels the record names — public/client, project-team, and
Developer Mode. In Developer Mode the subject changes to ARCHIOSK itself; the shape
does not.

## Why it matters

It decides what the interface is allowed to become. The stated preference is
"intent plus selected context plus conversation" over "a catalogue of permanent
technical controls" — which is why capability arrives through the Composer rather
than accumulating as console sprawl. Without this intent recorded, every new
capability has a natural pull toward becoming its own permanent button.

## What it enables

- One entry path to reason about, rather than one per capability.
- Capability growth without surface growth.
- The same interaction grammar at public, project and developer levels.
- The "composable tools-that-make-tools direction" the source record names —
  inspectability and recombination rather than a widening control panel.

## What it does not authorize

- **It does not authorize removing existing controls.** Preferring conversation to
  a control catalogue is a direction, not a licence to delete affordances people
  use. `CIC-PANEL` and `CIC-PAGE-TEMPLATE` govern surface changes.
- **It does not authorize routing a capability through the Composer** because that
  is more on-vision. Where a capability is governed elsewhere, that governance
  applies unchanged.
- **It does not authorize tool generation.** "Tools that make tools" is a
  *direction* here and remains unbuilt — `GO-TOOL-MAKING-01` is APPROVED as
  programme direction with the generation workflow explicitly not implemented.
- **It does not relax any authority boundary.** The source record is explicit that
  this direction preserves "provenance, reversibility, authority, and explicit
  approval boundaries."
