# ANA-002 — Generative coherence: one law, many truthful forms

- **ID:** ANA-002
- **TITLE:** Generative coherence — one law, many truthful forms
- **STATUS:** CURRENT

> **This record carries no implementation authority.** An analogy never creates an
> invariant. Nothing below authorizes a UI change, a visual mapping, a motion
> system, or a geometry.

## Analogy

> A single underlying language can produce many distinct, truthful expressions
> depending on context — as one poetic language yields many different voices, and as
> similar generative laws in nature yield many different forms. Coherence comes from
> the shared law, not from repeating the same shape.

## What it helps explain

Why ARCHIOSK should **not** pursue consistency by repeating identical cards,
identical borders, identical colours, identical panel layouts, or identical
Constructivist motifs — and why the same governed condition may legitimately look
and behave differently on two surfaces without the product becoming inconsistent.

The mental model has three parts:

1. **Stable generative invariants** — provenance, authority, evidence sufficiency,
   uncertainty, lineage, relationship, consequence.
2. **Local contextual pressures** — TPL/workspace role, task, project maturity, user
   posture, consequence level, current evidence state.
3. **Generated expression** — the combination determines the appropriate form,
   hierarchy, persistence, behaviour, interaction and emphasis *for that place*.

The useful consequence: **the same governed condition may manifest differently
across different surfaces without becoming inconsistent**, provided the law
producing it is the same one.

The concise form the Product Owner used: *one law, many truthful forms.* That phrase
is explanatory language, not doctrine. `GOV-P-002`'s own prohibited-drift clause
already warns that a slogan is not the governance.

## ARCHIOSK mapping

| Analogy element | ARCHIOSK element |
|---|---|
| The underlying language | Governed state: provenance, authority, evidence sufficiency, uncertainty, lineage, relationship, consequence |
| Dialect / local conditions | TPL and governed region, task, maturity, user posture, consequence level |
| The distinct expression produced | Hierarchy, persistence, placement, interaction boundaries, reversibility, emphasis, density, adjacency, visual weight on *that* surface |
| Two voices sharing one grammar | Two surfaces expressing the same condition differently and both being right |

Mapped only that far. The analogy says nothing about which form is correct in any
given place — that is a design judgement made against the local context, not derived
from the metaphor.

## Where it is useful

Orienting a designer or an agent before visual work, and in design review as the
companion to [VIS-003](VIS-003.md)'s question — *what governed condition, task
context, or usability constraint generated this form, and why is this expression
truthful here?*

**Where it should not be reached for:** any argument that a specific visual
treatment is required. This analogy explains why divergence can be legitimate; it
never nominates the form.

## DOES NOT MEAN

**Mandatory. Never empty.**

- **"Nature's geometry should be reproduced in the UI."** It does not follow. There
  are no hexagonal layout rules, no nature-derived geometry system, no fixed visual
  motifs drawn from natural form. The natural and poetic references are conceptual
  evidence for generative coherence, not UI specifications.
- **"Authority behaves like gravitational mass."** Discussed as a design probe only.
  It establishes no gravity system, no mass scoring, and no size mapping. There is
  no rule that a more authoritative object is larger, heavier or more central.
- **"Uncertainty should be shown as spatial constraint."** A probe, not a rule. No
  universal fading, blurring, shrinking or bounding of uncertain content is implied
  or permitted by this record.
- **"Supersession should be shown as displacement."** A probe, not a rule. No
  automatic motion, sliding, or animated replacement follows from it.
- **"Relationships should be shown as magnetic tension."** A probe, not a rule. It
  authorizes no force simulation, no attraction/repulsion layout, and no
  relationship graph rendering anywhere in the product.
- **"Therefore the interface may be physics-driven."** The metaphor must not become
  pseudo-physics. No physics simulation, no force equations, no geometric formulae
  are established here.
- **"Divergence needs no justification."** The opposite. Different forms are
  legitimate only when the *same* governed law and a *real* local difference
  produced them. Two surfaces differing for no reason is inconsistency, not
  generative coherence.
- **"Consistency mechanisms are now discouraged."** Shared macros, template
  families, the token system and parity checks remain correct and governed.
  `CIC-PAGE-TEMPLATE` v1.0's parity discipline is untouched.
- **"This is Bauhaus/Constructivist styling guidance."** It is not. It says nothing
  about red/black geometry, industrial treatment, skeleton graphics, or any visual
  vocabulary at all.
