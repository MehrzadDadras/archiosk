## Analogy

> The Composer is the service counter of the kiosk. GO is the intelligence and
> professional machinery working behind that counter. You speak to the counter; the
> machinery is what answers.

## What it helps explain

Why ARCHIOSK has **one** place to ask for things rather than one control per
capability — and why adding a capability does not usually mean adding a button. At
a counter, the range of things you can ask for grows without the counter changing
shape. It also explains why the same interaction feels consistent at public,
project-team and Developer Mode levels: the counter is the same, the staff behind
it are working on a different subject.

## ARCHIOSK mapping

| Analogy element | ARCHIOSK element |
|---|---|
| The kiosk | ARCHIOSK itself — see [VIS-001](VIS-001.md) |
| The service counter | The Composer (`CIC-COMPOSER` v1.0) |
| Staff and machinery behind the counter | GO — model-backed reasoning, evidence retrieval, tools, workflows |
| What you say at the counter | Intent, plus selected context, plus conversation |
| What you point at while asking | Selection — context only, never authorization ([GOV-P-001](../records/GOV-P-001.md)) |
| The back room you cannot walk into | Governed mutation paths, the Approval Gate, adjudication |
| Asking about the kiosk itself | Developer Mode — same counter, ARCHIOSK as the subject |

The source record states the path as
`USER → COMPOSER → GO → appropriate intelligence, evidence, tools and workflows`.

## Where it is useful

Orienting someone who has just met the product and is asking "where is the button
for X". Useful in design conversations about whether a new capability needs its own
surface. Useful for an agent deciding where a capability belongs.

**Not** useful for deciding what a capability is permitted to do — that is
governance, and the counter metaphor says nothing about it.

## DOES NOT MEAN

- **Does not mean the Composer is the only permitted entry point.** Files,
  Requirements, panels and menus are real surfaces with their own contracts.
  "Primary" in `CIC-DEVELOPER-MODE` means *primary toolbox for Developer Mode*, not
  *sole route into the product*.
- **Does not mean asking is receiving.** A counter takes requests; it does not
  approve them. Every governed mutation still passes its own authorization path.
  This is the single most likely over-extension of this analogy.
- **Does not mean GO is a person, or that its answers carry professional
  authority.** GO produces provisional, cited, adjudicable material. A Code or
  design conclusion is at best a supported interpretation requiring specialist
  confirmation — never `directly_verified` by GO.
- **Does not mean the counter remembers you.** Session and project scoping are
  governed; Developer context is project-filtered specifically to prevent
  cross-project leakage. A counter clerk who recognises you is not the model here.
- **Does not mean "one counter" is a UI law.** `CIC-COMPOSER`'s "One primary
  Composer" is about one canonical input per surface. That is a contract obligation
  with its own wording — it is not derived from this analogy, and this analogy
  cannot be cited in its place.
- **Does not mean everything behind the counter is available on request.**
  Unbuilt, deferred and NOT AUTHORIZED capability remains exactly that; the counter
  framing does not imply the machinery exists.

This analogy has **not** matured into a formal architectural term. "Composer" and
"GO" are formal product terms with their own contracts; "service counter" and
"kiosk" remain explanatory. Contrast **Airlock** and **Vestibule**, which have
matured into defined mechanisms with governed missions attached.
