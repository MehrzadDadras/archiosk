## Governing principle

**Learn how to investigate without learning what to believe.**

Prior projects may teach useful investigative approaches, recurring issue patterns may suggest where to look, earlier evidence relationships may suggest useful probes, and prior near-misses or successful investigations may improve attention. Past decisions remain contextual, attributable, and project-specific. Historical repetition does not establish present applicability, and current project evidence and authority govern the conclusion.

GO must preserve provenance and distinguish prior experience from current evidence.

## What the corpus may teach

Future governed patterns may suggest investigative moves such as:

- when a requirement changes, inspect dependent evidence;
- when sources conflict, check authority and revision context;
- when a commissioning gap appears, inspect upstream requirement and design alignment;
- when an operational condition changes, inspect cross-disciplinary consequences.

These are possible investigative patterns, not predetermined findings, conclusions, requirements, or answers.

## What the corpus must not teach

Do not turn historical experience into universal claims such as “this design solution is always correct,” “this specification always governs,” “this authority always interprets the issue this way,” or “because three prior projects did X, this project must do X.”

Historical experience may trigger investigation. It does not replace current evidence, current authority, scope, jurisdiction, or human adjudication.

## Canonical admission and surfacing boundary

The canonical specification permits only structured evidence-domain or check-type references, not outcome-shaped prose; strips outcome valence from reusable material; and requires every surfacing to retain full provenance. It guides where to look, never what to believe.

Any future cross-project promotion must pass generalization or abstraction, confidentiality and re-identification review, adversarial-party eligibility review, and explicit authorization. Detailed admission, partition, timing, and release mechanics remain unresolved. No project-private content enters a shared corpus merely because it proved useful.
