## Confirmed principles

- Responsibility may be distributed across multiple people, agents, disciplines, and workstreams.
- Delegation may itself create further bounded delegation.
- Delegated work remains connected to its originating purpose and authority.
- Ownership, responsibility, dependencies, readiness, and status remain inspectable.
- Central GO coordinates and supervises rather than blindly executing everything itself.
- Consequential gates remain subject to appropriate human authority.
- Parallel workstreams remain context-isolated where necessary while converging when dependencies require it.
- Delegation preserves provenance so the user can understand who or what acted, under which authority, and what result returned.

Current repository truth remains narrower: `Task` has no assignee or delegation fields, and governance explicitly records that no delegated-work-item concept or dedicated delegation object exists. This programme record does not reinterpret current orchestration, tasks, investigations, or agent tooling as implementation.

## Recursive delegation

A delegated task may safely produce bounded subtasks or specialist workstreams when necessary to complete the original objective. Recursion remains governed: no uncontrolled task spawning, authority expansion, or context leakage.

Each branch remains tied to its originating task, scope, authority, dependencies, expected result, and completion or return state.

## Relationship to River

[GO-RIVER-01](GO-RIVER-01.md) is related but distinct. A future delegation structure may behave like a River through paths such as task → subtask, dependency → readiness, result → downstream consequence, and blocked work → upstream attention.

Do not collapse the evidence River and delegation River into one undifferentiated relationship system unless a later authorized architecture investigation demonstrates that composition preserves their different semantics, provenance, and authority.

## Concurrent orchestration

**Concurrent preparation, synchronized service.**

The kitchen metaphor describes parallel specialist workstreams operating under different contexts, clocks, dependencies, and readiness states, then converging at the correct moment. It is architectural, not literal.

Preserve parallel specialist workstreams, context isolation, dependency-aware timing, readiness and quality gates, continuous supervision, and synchronized integration.

## Human governance

Distributed leadership does not confer autonomous institutional authority. GO may coordinate, propose, delegate within authorized bounds, and surface readiness or blockers. Legal, contractual, design, owner, security, billing, and other consequential authorities remain with the appropriate humans and governed systems.

Every delegation must preserve who or what acted, the scope and authority under which it acted, and the result returned. Existing governance already demonstrates that session-authenticated authorship and the authority that produced judgment content are not always equivalent; future delegation architecture must not flatten that distinction.
