## Confirmed direction

GO should eventually support design-build risk management including structured risk registers and Monte Carlo contingency analysis where appropriate.

Known Product Owner context includes Progressive Design-Build risk registers and contingency outputs such as:

- P50;
- P80;
- P90.

These labels preserve the intended output class. They do not establish a distribution model, confidence claim, acceptance threshold, or required contingency policy.

## Confirmed principles

- Risk inputs and assumptions remain inspectable.
- Simulations support human decision-making rather than generating false certainty.
- Risk likelihood and consequence inputs preserve provenance.
- Scenario inputs, model configuration, randomization controls, and changes should be reproducible where applicable.
- Risk-register entries should relate to project evidence where possible.
- Outputs must remain traceable to the input set, assumptions, model or engine version, and run identity that produced them.
- Weak, arbitrary, incomplete, or poorly supported inputs do not gain statistical validity merely by being simulated.
- Quantitative output does not itself authorize risk acceptance, contingency approval, design decisions, or project commitments.

## Current repository boundary

The implemented multimodal and work-product machinery can ingest, address, edit in a bounded way, cite, revise, issue, and export spreadsheet-shaped risk-register evidence. A `risk_register` WorkProduct is also available. These are real capabilities, but neither constitutes a permanent canonical Risk schema or Monte Carlo engine.

Current governance deliberately avoids forcing a permanent risk-record schema prematurely: risk-register rows are representable through existing spreadsheet regions and evidence items. The repository documents P50/P80/P90, contingency, dominant-driver identification, and rerun/compare as a future canonical Design-Manager case, not as implemented behaviour.

## Relationship to project evidence

Risk statements, assumptions, probabilities, consequences, mitigations, owners, and simulation outputs should cite or relate to governed project evidence where the evidence exists. Direct project evidence, human-entered assumptions, derived quantities, and simulated outputs must remain distinguishable.

[GO-RIVER-01](GO-RIVER-01.md) may eventually connect a risk or model input to supporting, contradicting, changing, or affected evidence. A relationship must not manufacture causal certainty or statistical independence.

## Relationship to Helix

[GO-HELIX-01](GO-HELIX-01.md) treats cost and schedule as consequential project strands whose convergence with requirements, design, procurement, commissioning, and operations may need testing. Risk intelligence may help investigate uncertainty and propagation across those strands, but it must not reduce Helix to a risk score or automated project-health percentage.

## Relationship to future situational attention

[GO-ADAPTIVE-ATTENTION-01](GO-ADAPTIVE-ATTENTION-01.md) may eventually use evidenced risk changes, dependencies, or uncertainty to guide attention. Simulation percentiles must not become automatic priority rankings, and historical risk results must not silently govern a changed present scenario.

## Programme boundary

This record does not authorize a Risk object, risk schema, probability distributions, correlation model, Monte Carlo engine, schedule or cost engine, contingency rule, quantitative UI, automated risk acceptance, or runtime changes. Existing risk-register evidence and WorkProduct machinery remain authoritative within their implemented bounds.
