## Episode framing

A project should preserve more than files and final artefacts. Its consequential story includes:

- what the team was trying to achieve;
- what changed;
- what mattered;
- key decisions and why they were made;
- near-misses;
- approaches that worked;
- unresolved tensions;
- relevant roles and contributions where appropriate;
- lessons worth carrying forward.

**project = episode**

**team = temporary cast**

**evidence = archive**

**decisions = plot turns**

**organizational memory = what survives after the cast moves on**

Informal project talk is often organizational oral history. GO should help convert that disappearing human memory into durable, attributable, evidence-linked organizational memory without inventing recollections or laundering unsupported narrative into truth.

## Retrieval direction

A future retrieval should support the class: “A similar issue happened before — here is what the team encountered, what they decided, and what mattered.”

Prior projects are not unquestioned precedent. Preserve the governing principle: **Learn how to investigate without learning what to believe.** Past experience remains contextual, evidence-linked, attributable, open to contradiction, and non-dogmatic.

## Project closure and re-entry

Project Memory should support project closure, later re-entry, onboarding into an old project, understanding why the current state exists, and recovering consequential history without rereading the entire corpus. A useful re-entry briefing explains the project story concisely while allowing supporting evidence, authority, uncertainty, and unresolved issues to be inspected.

Current session-level conversational context is explicitly not long-term Project Memory. This programme does not reinterpret ephemeral selection, chat continuity, or ordinary document storage as episode memory.

## Relationship to LAMS

[GO-LAMS-01](GO-LAMS-01.md) is the layered architecture for preserving different kinds and maturities of knowledge. Project Memory as Narrative reconstructs, at episode level, what happened and what mattered. They are related but not interchangeable, and neither absorbs the other.

## Relationship to River

[GO-RIVER-01](GO-RIVER-01.md) may help reconstruct how evidence led to decisions, decisions created consequences, changes propagated, and issues were resolved or remained open. A relationship alone does not prove narrative causation; chronology, provenance, authority, uncertainty, and contrary evidence remain necessary.

## Relationship to Composer and Voice

[GO-COMPOSER-01](GO-COMPOSER-01.md) and [GO-VOICE-01](GO-VOICE-01.md) may eventually let users ask natural historical questions such as “What happened here?”, “Why was this changed?”, “What nearly went wrong?”, “What did we learn?”, or “Has something similar happened before?”. Answers remain concise, evidence-grounded, attributable, and honest about insufficiency. Do not invent team memory unsupported by evidence.

## Cross-project intelligence boundary

Episode memory may eventually support cross-project learning, but retrieval must preserve project isolation, confidentiality, provenance, context, authority, and material differences between apparently similar situations. Repeated patterns do not become mandatory design rules.

The Experience Corpus is related but distinct and remains unauthorized. Its governed principle—learning where and how to investigate without importing outcome-shaped belief—does not itself authorize narrative cross-project retrieval. Any future promotion must obey confidentiality, re-identification, adversarial-party eligibility, and human-authorization controls.

## Programme boundary

This record does not authorize project-memory storage, narrative generation, closure workflows, cross-project retrieval, Experience Corpus promotion, UI, or runtime changes.
