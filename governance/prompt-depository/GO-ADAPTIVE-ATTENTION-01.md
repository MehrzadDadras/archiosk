## Core metaphors and principles

- The **gear metaphor** describes hierarchy, coupling, transmission of consequence, stability, and change velocity.
- The **compound-eye model** combines many passive or watchful facets with relatively few active facets.
- Most of the system may remain quietly observant while limited attention is actively allocated where consequence warrants it.
- Hierarchy is dynamic rather than fixed.
- Attention follows meaningful changes and dependencies rather than document order or discipline rank.
- Attention can move without losing awareness of what has receded.

## Attention survival

**Attention must survive.**

When active attention moves elsewhere, consequential context must not simply disappear. Prior relevant state remains recoverable; important unresolved conditions are not forgotten merely because another issue becomes more urgent; and attention changes preserve sufficient project memory and provenance for later re-entry.

The canonical deferred record also preserves attention survival through handoff across reviewers and sessions, bounded Investigation “beads” connected by intent, evidence, or dependency, selective context circulation, contextual defence against unrelated retrieval, and final human authority over significance.

## Dynamic hierarchy

Adaptive hierarchy may be influenced by consequence, dependency, rate of change, uncertainty, unresolved conflict, maturity mismatch, authority, schedule sensitivity, procurement sensitivity, and safety or operational significance.

Do not turn these factors into a universal scoring formula unless separately governed. The purpose is adaptive prioritization, not fake precision. Human judgment of significance remains authoritative.
