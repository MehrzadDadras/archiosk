## Confirmed principles

- Project strands may evolve at different velocities.
- Coordination is not proven merely because individual documents exist.
- GO should test relationships between strands, not inspect each strand in isolation.
- The expected degree of convergence depends on the maturity or stage actually being claimed.
- A strand may be individually mature while still failing to mesh with dependent strands.
- Change in one strand may require investigation of consequentially related strands.
- Uncertainty, missing evidence, contradiction, authority, and propagation are relevant to convergence testing.
- Helix is a conceptual and governing investigation model, not a universal numeric scoring system.

## Explicit prohibitions

- Do not reduce Helix to universal LOD scores.
- Do not create arbitrary project-health percentages.
- Do not impose fixed trade hierarchies.
- Do not invent universal maturity numbers.
- Do not use Helix to make automatic engineering or design corrections.
- Do not treat apparent non-convergence as proof of noncompliance without evidence and authority context.

## Exact prompt text

```text
A project consists of independently evolving but interdependent strands — disciplines, requirements, schedule, cost, procurement, commissioning, operations, authority, approvals, and other consequential project systems — moving toward delivery.

Coordination quality is demonstrated by how well consequential strands progressively mesh at the maturity being claimed.

**Spin is GO's governed process for testing that mesh.**
```
