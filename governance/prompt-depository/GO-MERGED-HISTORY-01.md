## Confirmed direction

GO should eventually provide a coherent history of consequential project evolution across documents, changes, decisions, findings, tasks, and other governed events rather than leaving chronology fragmented across separate registers.

## Confirmed principles

- A merged chronology does not erase, rewrite, or replace source-specific records.
- Event time, evidence time, issue time, and later interpretation remain distinguishable where relevant.
- Superseded records and earlier states remain inspectable.
- Corrections and later interpretations must not silently rewrite the time or meaning of the original event.
- The history should help users answer: What happened? When? What changed? Why? What followed from it?
- Chronological adjacency alone does not prove causation.
- Authority, provenance, uncertainty, and supersession remain visible when material to understanding an event.

## Current repository boundary

The implemented repository already retains a decentralized, non-destructive event record through `GovernanceLog` and object-specific histories such as Supersession and Requirement Adjudication chains. Current governance considers those records sufficient to reconstruct earlier direction, later refinement, and current accepted state for commissioning; it explicitly rejects the need for an event-sourcing rebuild.

A future merged history should therefore begin as a governed projection over authoritative records where composition is sound. It must not introduce a competing history store merely to create a timeline. Before any generic cross-event projection is built, event-specific field semantics must be audited rather than assumed uniform.

## Relationship to Project Memory

[GO-PROJECT-MEMORY-01](GO-PROJECT-MEMORY-01.md) preserves episode-level narrative reconstruction of what happened and what mattered. Merged History preserves event chronology across governed records. A narrative may use chronology as evidence, but narrative selection and interpretation are not the timeline itself. Do not collapse them.

## Relationship to River

[GO-RIVER-01](GO-RIVER-01.md) may expose relationships among evidence, decisions, changes, and consequences. River edges can help a user traverse what followed from an event, but a relationship does not by itself establish event order or causal certainty.

## Relationship to LAMS

[GO-LAMS-01](GO-LAMS-01.md) preserves different kinds and maturities of knowledge without flattening them. A future timeline may draw on those layers, but chronology is not the memory architecture and must not convert historical inference into present truth.

## Relationship to Living Knowledge

[GO-LIVING-KNOWLEDGE-01](GO-LIVING-KNOWLEDGE-01.md) concerns connected, evolving, contextual knowledge navigation. Merged History may become one chronological path through that knowledge, while preserving source identity, state, and authority.
